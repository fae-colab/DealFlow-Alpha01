# DealFlow — Founding Principles

## Evidence before claims
We do not claim that DealFlow is unique, superior, commercially validated, or financially effective until appropriate evidence supports those claims.

## The three evidence requirements
1. **Software that works.** The product must execute correctly under documented assumptions and pass repeatable tests.
2. **Better decision-making.** Recommendations must outperform credible alternatives or improve decisions in a measurable, independently reviewable evaluation. Passing unit tests is not evidence of better decisions.
3. **Measurable financial value.** Better decisions must translate into measurable incremental financial outcomes under a credible comparison design. Estimated commission is not realized commission; correlation is not causation.

## Product principles
- Evidence before assumptions and marketing claims.
- Functionality before appearance.
- Decision quality before AI complexity.
- Measurable outcomes before claims of value.
- Customer validation before fundraising.
- Preserve a clear distinction between synthetic demonstrations, user-entered assumptions, observed outcomes, and proven causal impact.

## Product question
Given everything happening in an agent's business today, where should limited attention go to create the greatest expected incremental commission?

DealFlow is intended as a decision-intelligence layer, not a CRM, lead score, task manager, dashboard, chatbot, or generic assistant. This positioning remains a hypothesis to test, not a claim of market differentiation.

## Development gates
1. Prove the software behaves as documented.
2. Test decision quality against strong baselines and experienced judgment.
3. Validate a costly, underserved customer problem and willingness to pay.
4. Measure financial impact with credible evidence.
5. Only then consider scaling and fundraising.

If evidence fails, reject or redesign the proposal. Do not manufacture evidence.
